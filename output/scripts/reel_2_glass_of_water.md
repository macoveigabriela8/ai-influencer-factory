## Why 73s and not the 45s in SOP Part 2

A 45s cut of this script scores **74** — below the rubric's own
do-not-post floor. The loss is structural, not sloppy: four criteria
(B1 enumeration, B2 three-branch, D1 full absolution, C3 share trigger)
only score at length, and Part 4 requires ≥90 for the first 10 posts.

The 30–60s band was justified **economically** ("Seedance bills flat, so
45s costs ~$9 against ~$31"), not on performance — Part 0 measures
duration↔plays at `r = +0.113`, weakly *positive*. So length was never
the protected variable; cost was. At 73s this bills ~$15: still half his
native length, still ~2× the shots on goal, and it clears the bar.

**Ceiling for this topic is 97.** D2 (topic stakes) caps at 8/11 —
the scale reserves 11 for death and grief, and scores ritual / invisible
burden at 8. His own #1 scored 8 there. A2 realistically caps at 8 too.

## The script

### CLIP 1 — HOOK + INSTRUCTION  (~12s, 29 words)

> Place a glass of water beside your bed tonight.
> Tomorrow morning you will understand why.
> Before you sleep, fill a clear glass with water.
> Hold it with both hands.

First two lines **verbatim from BIG01**. Never rewrite the hook.
"Hold it with both hands" is the strange specific detail — it is what
makes the instruction read as inherited rather than invented.

### CLIP 2 — THE WORDS + ENUMERATION  (~12s, 27 words)

> Say quietly: absorb what is heavy, remove what is not mine.
> Then sleep.
> In the morning, look at it before you speak to anyone.
> Three things can happen.

"Three things can happen" is load-bearing — it is the whole of B1.
An announced count tells the viewer where they are and how much is
left, which is why his enumerated reels hold. Eight words, +5 points.

### CLIP 3 — BRANCHES 1 & 2, alarming  (~14s, 34 words)

> First, small bubbles cling to the inside of the glass.
> The room was working hard. This is the most common.
> Second, the water is cloudy.
> That weight is older — months you never put down.

The bubbles branch is restored from BIG01 and is not optional: it is
**the most common outcome**, so without it the majority of viewers land
in no branch at all and the retention engine stalls.

### CLIP 4 — BRANCH 3 + ABSOLUTION  (~12s, 28 words)

> Third, it is still clear. Your space is light.
> Not all exhaustion comes from the body, my friend.
> You are not lazy, and you are not weak.

Branches run alarming → reassuring. The absolution is the beat people
screenshot; "you are not lazy, and you are not weak" is the explicit
blame-reframe the rubric asks for, and is what a compressed version
loses first.

### CLIP 5 — ABSOLUTION CLOSE + SHARE TRIGGER  (~11s, 25 words)

> You have been sleeping beside something you could not see.
> Send this to the person who sleeps eight hours
> and still wakes up exhausted.

The share trigger names a **specific person**, never "share this".
("Save this" scores zero — it is inversely correlated.) Twelve words
for 5 points; cutting it was the single worst trade in the 45s draft.

### CLIP 6 — PRODUCT BRIDGE  (~14s, 34 words)

> A glass of water shows you the weight. It cannot take it off you.
> Forty years taught me to live without it.
> Everything I know is inside The Quiet Between Thoughts.
> The link is in my profile.

⚠️ **Do NOT reuse `output/assets/seedance_07.mp4`.** It closes Reel B,
which is already published — appending it would end two consecutive
posts with the same twelve seconds, identical words *and* identical
picture, since it is one file rather than a re-performance. See CLAUDE.md:
his close is a fixed tail with a **freshly written head**, 7/7 distinct.

The pivot names the glass and its limit, which is what beat 7b actually
asks for. **"The link is in my profile" is the one invariant** — 7/7
verbatim across the BIG transcripts. Vary everything above it.
