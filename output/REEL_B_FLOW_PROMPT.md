@[CHARACTER] Speaks the following dialogue exactly as written. Do not omit, paraphrase, reorder, or add any words.

: If you dream about people who have already passed away, pay attention. It matters how they appeared.

In my tradition we do not say the dead come to visit us. We say the part of you that still loves them has something it has not finished saying.

So the question is never, was it really them. The question is how they appeared. Think back to last night, or to the last time. Do not force it. The image comes on its own. Most people try to remember the whole dream. Do not. One picture is enough.

If they were silent and simply present, saying nothing, nothing is wrong. That is memory settling. Longing, taking a shape you can hold. It is not a message. It is a settling.

If they spoke to you and you cannot remember the words, you already know what they would have said. You are not ready to say it back yet. Let it stay unsaid a while longer. It will keep.

If they appeared young again, healthy, before the illness, that is you letting go of the last image, the hardest one. That is grief moving forward, not backward. The body you last saw is not the body they are.

And if you could not see their face, that is not them, my friend. That is the part of yourself you buried with them. Look for what you stopped doing on the day they died.

When you wake from a dream like this, do one thing. Stay still. Do not reach for your phone. Give it thirty seconds of silence. Say their name out loud, once. The phone will take the dream from you before you have understood it. Thirty seconds. That is all it asks.

People carry guilt about these dreams. They think they are being haunted, or that they are going backwards. You are not going backwards. Grief is not a straight road, and it was never meant to be. Someone who truly loved you does not disappear completely. They stay in the only room that still has space for them.

Write their name in the comments. Just the name. Nothing else. Say it here, where someone will read it. And come back tomorrow and tell me how you slept.

If you want to learn how to listen to what life keeps trying to tell you, in dreams, in tiredness, in the days that feel heavy, I spent over forty years learning that in silence. Everything I know is inside The Quiet Between Thoughts. The link is in my profile.
