# REEL A — "The Glass of Water"
**Hook source:** Monk Chen #1 (4,047,769 plays)
**Word count:** 392 · **Runtime:** ~155s

```
[0:00–0:08]  HOOK  (verbatim)
Place a glass of water beside your bed tonight.
Tomorrow morning you will understand why.

[0:08–0:16]  AUTHORITY
In the temple where I trained, we did this every night for forty years.
Not for luck. To see what the room was holding.

[0:16–0:48]  INSTRUCTION
Before you sleep, fill a clear glass with clean water.
Not from a bottle. From the tap, cold.
Hold it in both hands for the length of one slow breath.
Then say quietly, only once: "Take what is not mine."
Place it on the floor — not the nightstand, the floor —
between your bed and the door.
That is the path everything travels while you sleep.
Then sleep normally. Do not look at it again tonight.

[0:48–1:32]  FOUR BRANCHES  ← retention engine
In the morning, before you speak to anyone, look at the glass.

If small bubbles cling to the inside of the glass —
the room was working hard. Ordinary tiredness, ordinary noise.
This is the most common.

If the water level has dropped —
you have been giving too much of yourself to someone.
The room took what you were already leaking.

If the water looks dull, flat, slightly grey —
this one is older. Weeks of carrying something you never put down.

And if the water is bright and clear, exactly as you left it —
your room is clean, my friend.
Sleep is not your problem. Something else is.

[1:32–1:48]  WARNING + ESCALATION
Never drink it. It did its work.
Pour it out under running water and rinse the glass.
If it is not clear after the first night, do it seven nights in a row.
Watch which night it changes.
That night will tell you more than the glass ever could.

[1:48–2:06]  ABSOLUTION
Not all exhaustion belongs to the body.
Some people wake up tired for years and blame themselves.
They think they are lazy, or weak, or broken.
They are not.
They have simply been sleeping beside something they could not see.

[2:06–2:20]  COMMENT + RETURN + SHARE
Write "tonight" in the comments if you will try this,
and come back tomorrow and tell me which of the four you found. I read them.
And send this to the person you know who never wakes up rested.

[2:20–2:35]  PRODUCT BRIDGE
But I will tell you the truth.
A glass of water can show you the weight. It cannot take it off you.
If you are tired of needing a ritual every time a day feels heavy —
I spent over forty years learning, in silence, how to live without them.
Everything I know is inside The Quiet Between Thoughts.
The link is in my profile.
```

**Upgrades over the original:** 4 branches instead of 3, and each maps to a *social* cause (giving too much, carrying something) rather than ambient "energy" — more personally implicating. Adds a **share trigger** (his had none). Adds a **placement rule** (floor, between bed and door) — the odd specificity that reads as authentic. Escalation now asks you to notice *which night* it changes, extending the loop to 7 days.

# REEL B — "Dreams of the Dead"
**Hook source:** Monk Chen #2 (1,384,721 plays)
**Word count:** 389 · **Runtime:** ~156s

```
[0:00–0:08]  HOOK  (preserved)
If you dream about people who have already passed away, pay attention.
It matters how they appeared.

[0:08–0:18]  AUTHORITY / REFRAME
In my tradition we do not say the dead come to visit us.
We say the part of you that still loves them
has something it has not finished saying.

[0:18–0:40]  FRAME
So the question is never "was it really them."
The question is how they appeared.
Think back to last night, or to the last time.
Do not force it. The image comes on its own.

[0:40–1:32]  FOUR BRANCHES  ← retention engine
If they were silent and simply present, saying nothing —
nothing is wrong. That is memory settling.
Longing, taking a shape you can hold.

If they spoke to you and you cannot remember the words —
you already know what they would have said.
You are not ready to say it back yet.

If they appeared young again, healthy, before the illness —
that is you letting go of the last image, the hardest one.
That is grief moving forward, not backward.

And if you could not see their face —
that is not them, my friend.
That is the part of yourself you buried with them.

[1:32–1:48]  INSTRUCTION + WARNING
When you wake from a dream like this, do one thing.
Stay still. Do not reach for your phone.
Give it thirty seconds of silence.
Say their name out loud, once.
The phone will take the dream from you
before you have understood it.
Thirty seconds. That is all it asks.

[1:48–2:06]  ABSOLUTION
People carry guilt about these dreams.
They think they are being haunted,
or that they are going backwards.
You are not going backwards.
Someone who truly loved you does not disappear completely.
They stay in the only room that still has space for them.

[2:06–2:22]  COMMENT CTA
Write their name in the comments. Just the name. Nothing else.
Say it here, where someone will read it.
And come back tomorrow and tell me how you slept.

[2:22–2:36]  PRODUCT BRIDGE
If you want to learn how to listen to what life keeps trying to tell you —
in dreams, in tiredness, in the days that feel heavy —
I spent over forty years learning that in silence.
Everything I know is inside The Quiet Between Thoughts.
The link is in my profile.
```

**Upgrades over the original:** his version had a weak, generic close. **"Write their name in the comments. Just the name."** is the strongest engagement mechanic in this whole set — near-zero friction, deeply personal, and it turns the comment section into a public memorial that other viewers scroll and add to. Comment sections like that keep generating sessions for weeks. Also adds the 30-second phone rule (a concrete, free, tonight-actionable instruction his version lacked) and a return-visit request.

# REEL C — "The Weekly Morning Routine"
**Hook source:** Monk Chen #3 (1,205,999 plays)
**Word count:** 395 · **Runtime:** ~158s

> **Deliberate change:** his version made medical claims — "activates thermogenesis," "forces your muscle cells to absorb sugar," "sticks to toxins." That is health misinformation and a takedown risk. This version keeps his hook and his *actual* winning angle — the stress/cortisol reframe he saves for the final third — and makes it the whole spine. No mechanism claims, no supplements, nothing a doctor should be saying.

```
[0:00–0:09]  HOOK  (preserved + curiosity gap added)
If a woman wants to lose fat as fast as possible,
following this weekly morning routine can help her reduce her waistline.
But not for the reason you think.

[0:09–0:20]  AUTHORITY / REFRAME
In forty years I have watched women do everything correctly
and change nothing.
The routine is not the food. It is the first ten minutes.

[0:20–1:00]  THE FIVE MORNINGS
Monday. Before anything else, warm water.
Sit with it. Do not stand, do not walk, do not scroll. Ten minutes.

Tuesday. Warm water with lemon, and one window open.
Cold air on your face while you drink.

Wednesday. Warm water, and before you drink,
three slow breaths out — longer than the breath in.

Thursday. Warm water, and you say out loud
the one thing you are dreading today. Say it once. Then drink.

Friday. Warm water, in silence, with your eyes closed.
No sound at all.

[1:00–1:40]  FOUR BRANCHES  ← the upgrade his version lacked entirely
Now. By the second week, notice which morning was hardest for you.
This is the part that matters.

If Wednesday was hardest — the breathing —
your body has forgotten how to exhale.
You are holding your stomach tight all day without knowing it.

If Thursday was hardest — saying it out loud —
what you carry is not in your body at all.
It is in what you are not saying.

If Friday was hardest — the silence —
you are using noise to outrun something.
And nothing moves while you are running.

And if Monday was hardest — simply sitting for ten minutes —
then you have not truly rested in a very long time, my friend.

[1:40–2:00]  ABSOLUTION
This is why women do everything right and nothing changes.
They clean the outside while the inside stays in survival.
A body that believes it is in danger will not let go of anything.
Not weight. Not tension.
Not the tightness in your face when you wake.
It is not discipline you are missing.
Your body is protecting you from a life
that has not felt safe in a long time.

[2:00–2:18]  COMMENT + SHARE
Write which morning was hardest — Monday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday —
in the comments, and I will tell you what it means.
And send this to the woman who does everything right
and blames herself anyway.

[2:18–2:38]  PRODUCT BRIDGE
If you want to learn how to calm the thing underneath —
the tension that keeps your body holding on —
I spent over forty years learning that in silence.
Everything I know is inside The Quiet Between Thoughts.
The link is in my profile.
```

**Upgrades over the original:** his scored **0 on diagnostic branching**. This version adds four branches keyed to *which morning was hardest*, which is self-selecting, personal, and produces a one-word comment. The share trigger ("the woman who does everything right and blames herself anyway") is written to be tagged at a specific friend. And it removes every medical claim while keeping the hook intact.
