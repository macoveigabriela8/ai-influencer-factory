Showering in the morning can be extremely dangerous and most people do it every day without even knowing. When you first wake up, your body is not fully activated yet. Your blood pressure is still low, your cortisol is rising, and your internal temperature is slowly trying to warm up for the day ahead. So when you suddenly hit your body with very hot water, first thing in the morning, it forces the heart and blood vessels to react faster than they should. For many people, this creates dizziness, weakness, nausea, or that strange feeling like you may almost black out for a moment. Morning showers can also remove the natural oils your skin built overnight, right when the body is trying to restore them. This may leave your skin drier, more sensitive, and more easily irritated throughout the day. But night showers often do the opposite. They calm the nervous system, they release tension from the body, they wash away the dirt, sweat, stress, and heaviness carried through the day. But this is not really about showers, it is about how most people live disconnected from their own nervous system. Small daily habits, done at the wrong time, quietly drain the body over the years.
