Anxiety isn't just worrying too much. It's a psychological and neurological state where the brain becomes locked in survival mode, constantly scanning for threats, real or imagined. For those who live with it, anxiety feels like being trapped in a storm of racing thought, restless energy and a body that never fully relaxes. This is not weakness and it is not simply overthinking. It is the nervous system misfiring, treating ordinary moments as danger and turning daily life into a silent battlefield. At the centre of this response is the brain's alarm system. When it becomes overactive, even small stresses are experienced as threat. The part of the brain meant to calm fierce struggles to regain control. The body prepares to survive. Breathing grows shallow, muscles tighten, the heart stays alert. This is why anxiety is felt in the body as much as in the mind. Often the roots of this pattern reach back to early life. In unstable or unpredictable environments, the body learnt to stay watchful, reading tone, mood and silence for signs of danger. What once protected the child becomes exhaustion in the adult. Anxiety is not brokenness. It is adaptation. It is the nervous system saying, I Learned this to keep you safe. Healing does not come from fighting the system, but from slowly teaching it that survival is no longer required.
